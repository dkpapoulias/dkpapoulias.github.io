My research focuses on **theoretical astroparticle physics and phenomenology**, with strong connections to **neutrino physics, dark matter, and nuclear theory**. A central theme of my work is to translate particle-physics models into experimentally testable signatures, exploiting the complementarity of different energy scales, scattering channels, nuclear targets, and detector technologies. These probes provide an important complement to high-energy collider searches, offering sensitivity to light states and weakly coupled new physics that can be difficult to probe at colliders.

---

## Coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering

Coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering (CEνNS) has developed into a precision tool for neutrino, electroweak, nuclear, and beyond-the-Standard-Model physics. My work in CEνNS spans the period from the first **COHERENT** observation to reactor measurements with **CONUS+** and the recent observation of solar CEνNS in **XENONnT, PandaX-4T, and LZ**.

My main directions include:

- **CEνNS across different neutrino sources:** combined phenomenological analyses of stopped-pion, reactor, and solar-neutrino measurements, exploiting complementary targets, spectra, and detector technologies.
- **Precision electroweak probes:** determinations of the weak mixing angle, neutron radii and neutron skins, including the impact of nuclear structure, quenching, and detector response.
- **Searches for new physics:** non-standard and generalized interactions, neutrino electromagnetic properties, light mediators, sterile states, and other departures from the Standard Model.

## Dark matter direct detection & the neutrino fog

Dark matter direct-detection experiments such as **XENONnT, LZ, and PandaX-4T** increasingly operate simultaneously as dark matter and neutrino observatories. My research investigates this interplay, from neutrino backgrounds and the emergence of the neutrino fog to new dark matter production and interaction mechanisms. I also explore the potential of **DarkSPHERE** and other low-threshold gaseous detectors for light dark matter and non-standard dark matter–nucleus interactions.

An additional direction is the search for **artificially produced dark matter at accelerator facilities**, where intense proton beams can generate light dark-sector particles that can subsequently be detected through their scattering in nearby neutrino detectors.

Current directions include:

- **Neutrino fog and new physics:** understanding the transition from the conventional neutrino floor to the neutrino fog, and how new neutrino interactions and light mediators can modify its morphology.
- **Sub-GeV and boosted dark matter:** extending searches beyond the conventional WIMP paradigm through low thresholds, alternative production mechanisms, and boosted dark matter scenarios.
- **Dark matter at intense neutrino sources:** exploiting **ESS, J-PARC, and CSNS** to produce and search for light dark matter with forthcoming CEνNS detectors.
- **Dark matter–nucleus interactions:** studying interactions beyond the conventional spin-independent and spin-dependent limits through non-relativistic effective field theory, with particular attention to nuclear responses and target complementarity.

## Neutrino properties & new physics across energy scales

My neutrino phenomenology extends from solar and reactor neutrinos to accelerator beams, using complementary sources and scattering channels to probe fundamental neutrino properties and interactions beyond the Standard Model. In addition to CEνNS, I study elastic neutrino–electron scattering (EνES) as a theoretically clean and complementary probe of new physics.

At accelerator energies, I have investigated the physics potential of the **DUNE Near Detector**, including generalized neutrino interactions through EνES and the production of new fermionic states through neutrino up-scattering on electrons and nuclei. Together with solar-neutrino measurements in **XENONnT, PandaX-4T, and LZ**, reactor experiments, and spallation sources, this provides sensitivity to new physics over widely different energies and momentum transfers.

Specific directions include:

- **Neutrino electromagnetic properties:** magnetic and electric dipole moments, transition moments, millicharges, charge radii, and related electromagnetic interactions.
- **Generalized and non-standard interactions:** scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, axial-vector, and tensor interactions probed through nuclear and electron scattering.
- **Sterile and new fermionic states:** oscillation searches, transition electromagnetic interactions, and neutrino up-scattering into new states.
- **Neutrino decay:** invisible neutrino decay using solar-neutrino data and the combined information from CEνNS and EνES.

## Dark sectors & feebly interacting particles

A complementary part of my research concerns light and feebly interacting particles whose signatures can be searched for across neutrino, reactor, accelerator, and dark matter experiments. I study dark photons and other light mediators, including U(1)′ scenarios ranging from tree-level B−L to loop-level Lμ−Lτ realizations, as well as axion-like particles (ALPs) and theoretically motivated dark-sector extensions of the Standard Model. These probes provide an important complement to high-energy collider searches, offering sensitivity to light states and small couplings that can be difficult to probe at colliders.

My main directions include:

- **Light mediators and new gauge interactions:** phenomenology of light scalar and vector mediators using CEνNS, neutrino–electron scattering, and dark matter direct-detection experiments.
- **Dark hypercharge:** low-scale U(1)X constructions constrained by anomaly cancellation and tested through neutrino and direct-detection data.
- **Axion-like particles (ALPs):** production and detection at reactor experiments through Primakoff, Compton-like, and nuclear processes.
- **Hidden photons and dark matter:** light hidden-photon scenarios, including connections to low-scale string constructions and electronic-recoil searches in experiments such as XENON1T.

## Nuclear structure for neutrino & dark matter searches

Nuclear structure provides the bridge between fundamental particle interactions and the signals measured in neutrino and dark matter detectors. My work incorporates realistic nuclear calculations into phenomenological analyses, both to improve theoretical predictions and to determine what information about nuclei can itself be extracted from scattering data.

This includes:

- **Nuclear responses and precision CEνNS:** form factors, neutron distributions, neutron radii and skins, together with coherent, incoherent, and inelastic neutrino–nucleus scattering.
- **Nuclear many-body methods:** calculations using the Nuclear Shell Model, QRPA, and MQPM, including hybrid approaches for nuclear excitations and neutrino–nucleus reactions.
- **Dark matter nuclear responses:** nuclear structure functions beyond the conventional spin-independent and spin-dependent limits, including the operator structure of non-relativistic effective field theory and the complementarity of different target nuclei.
